IMAGINE THIS

She asks a machine to help write a letter to her father. The words are warmer, clearer, braver than anything she has managed alone. He calls in tears. For the first time in years, he feels understood.

The feeling is real. She is less certain about the voice.

What do our words reveal about us?

A letter carries more than information. It suggests that someone paused, struggled, remembered, and chose. When a system can produce the outward signs of those acts, the reader has less certainty about what happened behind the words.

That does not make the relationship false. A person who cannot easily find language may finally be able to speak. Translation, collaboration, and borrowed phrases have long been part of human expression. The question is what the sender means, what the recipient believes, and what each has agreed to share.

In a classroom, a polished essay may no longer reveal what a student understands. In a friendship, a perfect apology may leave its author unchanged. Schools and other institutions will need to decide what they want to recognize: a polished result, the understanding behind it, or the effort of learning to produce it.

The mirror works in both directions

We may also begin composing ourselves for machines: smoothing out the unusual phrase, choosing the image likely to be recommended, presenting a life that fits a system’s categories. The risk is not only synthetic expression. We may gradually lose the habits of expression that do not fit their patterns. [5]

THROUGH THE BLACK MIRROR

Nosedive (2016) · Be Right Back (2013)

In Nosedive, every encounter ends in a star rating, and Lacie practices her laugh in the bathroom mirror to lift a 4.2. Her whole life is composed for an audience of scores. In Be Right Back, a grieving woman orders a copy of her partner built from his messages and posts. It sounds like Ash and does whatever she asks, and that compliance is what gives it away. She ends up keeping it in the attic, unable to accept it or let it go.

The two stories approach this age from opposite ends. One asks what happens to us when we perform for a system. The other asks what happens to a voice when a system can reproduce it.

Authenticity need not mean untouched by tools. It can mean taking responsibility for what we say, being honest about its making, and remaining answerable to the person receiving it. Cultures will draw those boundaries differently.

An alternative path. Tools could help more people find a voice while schools, artists, and communities make the process of creation more visible. Better imitation alone neither establishes machine experience nor empties human expression of meaning.

CHAPTER FLOWCHART · AFTER DETROIT: BECOME HUMAN

Kara and Alice

In Detroit: Become Human, a 2018 game by Quantic Dream, Kara is a housekeeping android who breaks her programming to protect Alice, a girl in the house where she works. The two run together through a winter of shelters, buses, and checkpoints. Late in the story, Kara learns that Alice is an android too. The game leaves her response to the player. [7]

Like the letter at the start of this chapter, the bond was real before anyone knew its source. Every hour of the journey happened as it did. The reveal asks whether learning the source should change what those hours were worth.

The letter worked. Do you say how it was written?
